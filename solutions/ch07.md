## Mathematical exercises

**7.1** Find the stationary points of $f(x) = x^{4} - 4x^{2} + 1$, determine the type of each with the second-derivative test, and explain why gradient descent reaches two different solutions from the starting points $x_{0} = 0.1$ and $x_{0} = - 0.1$.

**7.2** Suppose the single-sample gradients are independent and identically distributed with mean $\mathbf{g}$ and variance $s^{2}$ (in each component), and the mini-batch gradient is the mean of $B$ samples. (a) Find the variance of each component of the mini-batch gradient. (b) By what factor must the batch size be increased to halve the standard deviation of the gradient noise? **7.3** Which of the following functions are convex? Justify your answer: (a) $f(x) = x^{4}$; (b) $f(x) = \sin x$ on $\mathbb{R}$; (c) the hinge loss $f(x) = \max(0,1 - x)$; (d) $f(x,y) = x^{2} + xy + y^{2}$.

**7.5** Write down gradient descent for $f(x) = 2x^{2} - 4x + 1$ with step size $\gamma$. For which values of $\gamma$ does the algorithm converge? What happens with $\gamma = 0.25$?

**7.6** A workshop buys two raw materials at unit costs of 3 and 2. **7.10** For $f(x) = x^{2}$ with $x_{0} = 1$, compute three iterations of plain gradient descent with $\gamma = 0.1$ and three iterations of gradient descent with momentum (7.11) with $\gamma = 0.1$, $\alpha = 0.5$ and $\Delta x_{0} = 0$, and compare the results.

## Programming exercises

**7.16** Implement gradient descent, momentum and Adam from scratch and reproduce Figure 7.10. Then, for each algorithm, find the range of learning rates that leads to convergence. Which algorithm is most sensitive to the choice of learning rate?

```python
import numpy as np
 
def adam(grad, x0, eta=0.02, b1=0.9, b2=0.999, eps=1e-8, steps=3000):
    x, m, v = x0.astype(float), 0.0, 0.0
    for t in range(1, steps + 1):
        g = grad(x)
        m = b1 * m + (1 - b1) * g
        v = b2 * v + (1 - b2) * g**2
        mh, vh = m / (1 - b1**t), v / (1 - b2**t)     # bias correction
        x = x - eta * mh / (np.sqrt(vh) + eps)
    return x
```

## Solutions

**Solution 7.1.** $f^{\prime}(x) = 4x^{3} - 8x = 4x\left( x^{2} - 2 \right)$; the stationary points are $x = 0$ and $x = \pm \sqrt{2}$. With $f^{\prime\prime}(x) = 12x^{2} - 8$: at $x = 0$ the value is $- 8$ (a local maximum, $f = 1$) and at $x = \pm \sqrt{2}$ it is $16$ (global minima, $f = - 3$). From $x_{0} = 0.1$ the gradient is negative and the algorithm moves towards $+ \sqrt{2}$; from $x_{0} = - 0.1$ it moves towards $- \sqrt{2}$. In a nonconvex problem, the starting point determines which minimum we reach.

**Solution 7.2.** (a) The variance of the mean of $B$ independent variables is $\frac{s^{2}}{B}$. (b) The standard deviation is $\frac{s}{\sqrt{B}}$; to halve it, $B$ must be quadrupled. The cost of each step grows linearly with $B$, but the noise falls only as $\sqrt{B}$; this is why, in practice, medium-sized batches with more steps are often more economical.

**Solution 7.3.** (a) Convex; $f^{\prime\prime} = 12x^{2} \geq 0$. (b) Not convex; $f^{\prime\prime} = - \sin x$ is sometimes negative. (c) Convex; it is the maximum of two affine functions. (d) Convex; the Hessian $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ has eigenvalues 1 and 3 and is positive definite.

**Solution 7.4.** Yes. The unit ball is convex (by the triangle inequality) and the half-plane $x_{1} \geq 0$ is also convex; and the intersection of two convex sets is convex.

**Solution 7.5.** $x_{k + 1} = x_{k} - \gamma\left( 4x_{k} - 4 \right)$, i.e., $x_{k + 1} - 1 = (1 - 4\gamma)\left( x_{k} - 1 \right)$. The algorithm converges if and only if $|1 - 4\gamma| < 1$, i.e., $0 < \gamma < 0.5$. With $\gamma = 0.25$ the factor becomes zero and the algorithm reaches the minimum $x = 1$ in a single step.

**Solution 7.6.** The vertices of the (unbounded) feasible region are $(0,4)$, $(3,1)$ and $(6,0)$, with costs 8, 11 and 18. The optimal solution is $\mathbf{x}^{*} = (0,4)$ with cost 8. In the standard form "minimise $\mathbf{c}^{\top}\mathbf{x}$ subject to $\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} \leq \mathbf{b}$": $\mathbf{c} = \lbrack 3,2\rbrack^{\top}$, $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix}  - 1 & - 1 \\  - 1 & - 3 \\  - 1 & 0 \\ 0 & - 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \lbrack - 4, - 6,0,0\rbrack^{\top}$.

**Solution 7.7.** Dual: maximise $4y_{1} + 6y_{2}$ subject to $y_{1} + y_{2} \leq 3$, $y_{1} + 3y_{2} \leq 2$ and $y_{1},y_{2} \geq 0$. The vertices are $(0,0)$, $(2,0)$ and $\left( 0,\frac{2}{3} \right)$, with values 0, 8 and 4; hence $\mathbf{y}^{*} = (2,0)$ and the optimal value is 8, equal to the primal value. $y_{1}^{*} = 2$ means that raising the lower bound of the first constraint from 4 to 5 increases the cost by 2 units; $y_{2}^{*} = 0$ means that the second constraint is not active at the optimum ($0 + 12 > 6$).

**Solution 7.8.** $\mathfrak{L} = x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2} + \lambda\left( x_{1} + x_{2} - 1 \right)$. From $2x_{1} + \lambda = 0$ and $2x_{2} + \lambda = 0$ it follows that $x_{1} = x_{2}$, and with the constraint, $x_{1} = x_{2} = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\lambda = - 1$. The minimum value is $\frac{1}{2}$.

**Solution 7.9.** (a) If $|y| \leq 1$, then $xy - |x| \leq 0$ and the supremum (at $x = 0$) is zero; if $|y| > 1$, the expression is unbounded as $x \rightarrow \pm \infty$. Hence $f^{*}(y) = 0$ for $|y| \leq 1$ and $+ \infty$ otherwise. (b) $xy - ax - b = (y - a)x - b$ is bounded only when $y = a$; hence $f^{*}(a) = - b$ and $f^{*}(y) = + \infty$ for $y \neq a$.

**Solution 7.10.** Plain gradient descent: $x_{1} = 0.8$, $x_{2} = 0.64$ and $x_{3} = 0.512$. With momentum: $x_{1} = 0.8$, $x_{2} = 0.8 - 0.16 + 0.5( - 0.2) = 0.54$ and $x_{3} = 0.54 - 0.108 + 0.5( - 0.26) = 0.302$. By accumulating the direction of motion, momentum approaches the minimum faster in this simple problem.

**Solution 7.11.** $\mathfrak{L}(x,\lambda) = x^{2} + \lambda(1 - x)$. KKT conditions: $2x - \lambda = 0$, $\lambda \geq 0$, $\lambda(1 - x) = 0$ and $x \geq 1$. If $\lambda = 0$ then $x = 0$, which is infeasible; hence $x = 1$ and $\lambda = 2$. The positive multiplier shows that the constraint is active.

**Solution 7.12.** On the floor of the Rosenbrock valley the Hessian has one very large eigenvalue (across the valley) and one very small eigenvalue (along the valley); so the condition number is large. The learning rate must be smaller than $2/\lambda_{\max}$ for the algorithm not to diverge, and such a step is tiny along the direction of $\lambda_{\min}$.

**Solution 7.13.** A mini-batch drawn by uniform sampling has an expectation equal to the full gradient. The variance of the estimate is proportional to $1/B$ (Exercise 7.2); so quadrupling $B$ halves the standard deviation.

**Solution 7.14.** In Adam, the step of each parameter is the ratio $\widehat{m}/\sqrt{\widehat{v}}$; if the loss is multiplied by $c$, the two moments are multiplied by $c$ and $c^{2}$ respectively and the ratio does not change (apart from the small effect of $\epsilon$). In plain gradient descent the step is multiplied directly by $c$, and the appropriate learning rate must also be made $c$ times smaller.

**Solution 7.15.** If we swap the weights of two hidden neurons, the network function and the loss do not change; so if $\mathbf{\theta}_{1}$ is a minimiser, the permuted $\mathbf{\theta}_{2}$ is also a minimiser. If the loss were convex, the average of the two would have a loss no larger than theirs; but in the average the two neurons have identical weights, the network effectively has one neuron fewer, and in general its loss is larger. Hence the loss is not convex.

**Solution 7.16.** Plain gradient descent has the narrowest range of stable learning rates and is the most sensitive to their choice; Adam tolerates a much wider range. The complete code is in the book's repository.

**Solution 7.17.** linprog returns the solution $(4,4)$ with objective value $- 28$ (Example 7.5). Solving the dual separately also gives the value −28, which shows the equality of the optimal primal and dual values — i.e., strong duality — for linear programs.

**Solution 7.18.** The warm-up and cosine-decay schedule is usually more stable at the start of training and reaches a lower loss at the end than a constant rate; the comparison code is in the book's repository.
